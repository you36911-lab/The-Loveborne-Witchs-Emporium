## The game in one paragraph

A cozy witch-shop deduction game. Customers describe a need (never the recipe name) with a non-verbal cue in brackets and a coloured aura. The player works out the one right recipe from the books, lays it out at the altar (ingredients, candle, tarot card, the right day and hour) and gives it to them. Results come back later as stories and as stars on nightly receipts.

## Time and calendar

- One day is one session, split into four Magic Hours: Sunrise, Noon, Twilight, Nightfall.
- **No weather and no daily moon phase** (removed: too complicated for what they gave). A year is 72 days. What is left of the sky is calendar days, like the festivals: a **full moon every sixth day**, each with its own name, and a few **sky events** (Clear starry night, Shooting stars, Rainbow, Lunar Eclipse, Blue Moon).
- The game runs **Day 1 to Day 75**, Samhain to Samhain. Full moons fall on Days 6, 12, … 72 (Beaver Moon first); a recipe can ask for any full moon ("Full Moon") or one by name. Clear starry nights are Days 6, 15, 30, 36, 42, 44, 48, 53, 54, 56, 57, 58, 60, 64, 66 and 72 (Nightfall only). Sabbats: Samhain 1–3 and 73–75, Yule 10–12, Imbolc 19–21, Ostara 27–29, Beltane 37–39, Litha 46–48, Lughnasadh 55–57, Mabon 64–66.
- Festivals stay **three days** each (decided after weather and moon phases were removed): every order is made during the visit, and Ostara alone has 7 recipes that need it, so one-day festivals would crowd those days and make a missed festival recipe wait a whole year.
- Time moves on by itself only when a customer has left and nobody else is due that hour (AFTER_LEAVE_MS, 1.5s after they go): the light changes to the next hour, and the bell then calls the next customer. Not at Nightfall, where the bell closes the night. Otherwise time never moves by itself. The four hours still exist underneath (recipes use them) but are not labelled; the light in the shop shows the hour.
- The **bell** brings the next customer, and nobody comes in without it: someone due this hour comes straight in; otherwise it moves time to the next hour with a customer (who then comes in) or a letter, and closes the night at Nightfall. While someone is at the counter, or a customer is still saying their goodbye, it only shakes. (A prototype jump rings it for you.)
- Quiet hours show a mood line at the top of the scene. Post comes first: when an email is about to arrive (or its card is up), the mood line and the tutorial's thought wait for it.
- Over the year: 19 quiet hours with no customer or letter, left as breathing room. Week one has none.

## Customers and orders

- Regulars (Hester, Edmund, Dottie, Goldie, Rosalind, Morwenna, Felix, Ivy, Noor, Sam) have story arcs. Walk-ins order every other recipe exactly once over the year.
- Every order has exactly one right recipe; a different recipe fails.
- Week-one orders always say what kind of thing they want (a charm, something to spread on toast = butter, something to look into = hand mirror, and so on).
- **Every order is made while the customer waits.** Nobody says they'll come back for it: each visit is placed on a day and hour when its recipe can be made, a regular's visits keep their story order, nobody comes twice in a day, and no order brings 5 or more new parts. A few hours are shared with a walk-in. In week one Edmund comes at Nightfall, Goldie on Wednesday at Noon, and Morwenna's first order is The Tidal Heart (made with the sea water she brings); Wayfarer's Ward (Wednesday · Twilight) is her Day 19 visit, a Wednesday. There is no button to start an order.
- **Making and giving**: at the altar, ringing the **silver bell** on the table (the designer's altar bell, assets/scene/altar-bell.png, between what is laid out and the card) makes the order; it glows faintly when everything is ready, rings out (heard) and then slides the view back up to the counter, where it waits as a wrapped parcel (pink ribbon, a handwritten tag with the recipe name) between the till and the notebook. Clicking the parcel hands it over.
- **Sizes: cupboards vs the altar**: two size tables. In a cupboard everything is drawn large enough to tell apart (ITEM_SIZE, with each cupboard's own enlargement; the Wood & branches, Buttons and Findings & tools cupboards 1.6×). On the altar things stand true to life beside each other (REAL_SIZE, 1 = a 20 cm potion bottle): a sachet of brown sugar is small beside a bottle of water, a jar of herbs is about a hand tall, a bag of flour is big; gems drawn larger than life (0.42) so they can be told apart. On the altar nothing else shrinks them.
- **Cupboards**: an open cupboard shows its things large enough to tell apart (56% of the room wide; things 1.3× their old size, herbs 1.8× and always four to a row, fruit, spices, resins and critters 1.6×, the pantry and oils 1.45×, candles 1.08×; bottles on the water and oil shelves centred, each row as tall as what stands in it, short wide things (butter, beeswax) at least 0.62 of a bottle in the cupboard; 72% for the tarot cards), and always starts below the ceiling. Its things stand against the cupboard's own wooden back (assets/scene/cabinet-back.jpg, the middle of the designer's cupboard picture, upscaled), with no shelf planks drawn, framed by the cupboard picture's own wooden edges, with no title and nothing between the frame and the back. Bottled waters show how many are left as a pink badge on the bottle's shoulder, like the unread count on the computer. Things with a picture show no name label (the picture says what it is; hovering shows the name); only things without a picture yet are labelled. The same on the table. Making again before giving replaces it; it goes if the customer leaves.
- "I don't know how to make this" needs a second tap.
- **Popups**: every popup (books, diary, email, receipts, the end of the day, the new-email card, an open cupboard, a result at the counter) sits in the middle of the screen and closes when you click outside it (or with Esc), so none of them has a Close or × button. Only the tutorial note keeps its ×, since it is not a popup. Clicking outside the end-of-day summary goes on to the next day, as its button does.
- No order brings 5 or more parts the player hasn't met yet (except the Day 6 Beaver Moon stretch goal).
- **Results**: a regular tells their own result on their next visit, on its own screen before the new request. A walk-in comes back themselves in the next quiet hour (or after two days, before the next customer) to say how it went, in their own voice and about the right person. A perfect order needs no report; the customer's passing hint is the thanks. They stay at the counter to say it in their own balloon long enough to read it (PARTING_MS, 4.5s after the words finish), then leave by themselves; the bell only shakes meanwhile ("Let them finish first."), and the next customer waits until then.
- **Advice seekers** ask a quick question ("which herb…?"). They fill quiet hours in week one. The answer is typed: from two letters, names of the asked-for kind (herbs, crystals) are suggested. Only an exact name counts (capitals, spaces and accents aside); a misspelt or unknown name does nothing but bring up a thought as a subtitle ("Did I spell that right?"). Once their words have finished and the name box has come up, the witch's thought runs as the subtitle and narrows it to the right kind of thing ("A crystal that does just that… I've read about one somewhere.").
- **Names**: nobody at the counter is labelled, walk-in or regular. Who they are comes out in what they say and do; a regular says their own name in their first line. (The receipts and the diary still note who it was; a receipt gives a walk-in by their own name, from "names" in story.js, chosen to fit their picture.)
- **Speech balloon**: everything a customer says and does sits in one balloon just right of their face, its tail pointing at them: the cue in brackets first, then their words. The buttons sit below it. When someone new speaks, the balloon floats up and fades in, after the customer's picture has faded in (1.2s, then a 0.2s pause; the balloon takes 0.8s). A customer leaving fades out faster (0.45s); the mood line at the bottom floats in the same way. The altar's reminder reads "They asked: …".
- **Tutorials happen once**: the first order and the first advice question each get a numbered tutorial note during that visit only. Later visits carry no instructions, just what the customer says and the buttons. Each note has a × that puts it away for good. There is no "Skip tutorials"; a replay (a new game after one that got past Day 2, TUTORIAL_LAST_DAY) has no tutorial. Jumping with the prototype bar counts every tutorial whose first visit comes earlier as seen.

## Grading and feedback

- Grades (Perfect, Good, Clumsy, Failed) are internal; the player sees stars on receipts and reviews. Rating = average of the last 20, counted as each sale is rung up.
- **Reviews**: every receipt becomes a review in **My Shop** as it is rung up (decided 2026-11-11, so a hint can help the very next order: the first customer's High Priestess hint is there before Hester's rune), on the computer, read by day (Day 1, Day 2...) or all at once, with the rating above. A customer who got just what they needed writes their own review (`review` in story.js; new writing, not in the design document yet) and adds what they let slip as a P.S. (the hint). Other grades write a short stock line that fits their stars.
- Test crafts are made **only from the Recipe Book** ("Test craft this" on a recipe lays out what was written and tests it); there is no test button at the altar. If the bell can't make anything yet (no order, no recipe chosen, nothing laid out), it gives a little shake and says why. Test crafts are unlimited; every 5th imperfect one adds a 1-star review (from a neighbour: the street smells of burnt herbs). Failed tests show as **five black candles** on the designer's candelabra, standing on the altar right of the tarot card (assets/candles/test-candles-0…5.png, the number lit; dimmed a little to the room's light like the books): each imperfect test puts one out; when all five are out the candelabra stands dark for a moment, the star is lost, and all five light again. Each candle going out is heard: a soft whoosh of breath and the snuff of the flame. The candelabra stands a little back on the cloth, behind the card. A perfect test says so plainly ("Done") and writes the recipe into the Recipe Book.
- **Crafting**: a test craft (from the Recipe Book) lays out what was written, then plays the crafting on the altar: what is laid out drifts together and turns while a soft light rises behind it and little pink stars burst from the middle, Making an order for real (the silver bell) plays the same crafting before the view goes up to the counter. Nothing can be clicked meanwhile.
- **The phone**: test craft results come to a phone lying on the altar, right of the candle stand (the designer's phone2 picture, about 15 cm long beside the things on the table). After the crafting, a black candle goes out if the test isn't perfect, then the phone chimes ("New Notification 057") and shows a badge with the results not looked at yet (the same badge as the computer's; the phone does not glow for it, only on hover like everything else). Clicking it opens the phone: drawn as a real phone (dark bezel, notch, status bar with the day, home bar, 9:18), on its **Test Craft** app (title bar like the computer's apps; the bottle icon with a sparkle inside). The app scrolls with no scrollbar showing. It keeps the last result.
- Test-craft feedback (on the phone): the recipe's name, then how many ingredients are right (**3/6 ingredients**; no Overall count and no grade line such as "Nothing. It's just a pretty thing now."), then the clues grouped under their label (Ingredients, Candle, Card, Timing, Came through), all on one left edge. The labels are small headings in the shop app's rounded font (Fredoka, deep pink). Word-bank words are dotted and collected here as anywhere. A badge beside the name shows the grade: Perfect, Nearly, Clumsy or Failed (test crafts only; deliveries still never show it).
  - **Clues grow with each wrong try at the same recipe.** First try: only which shelves are short or over ("2 more from Herbs & flowers.", "One from Pantry doesn't belong."), and the card line only says the card is missing or wrong. From the second try: each missing one is pictured by its **look** ("One more is needed from Fruits. Picture it: small, red and seedy."), each wrong one is explained, and the card's blended feel is added.
  - Lines name the **shelf**, never the wrong ingredient ("The one from Herbs & flowers: the cleansing is right, but it works on the heart. This needs one that works on the home.").
  - A theme is only called missing if nothing in the mix brings it.
  - A wrong or missing card gets the right card's blended feel, without naming keywords ("feels like a still mind that knows what is real without being told").
- Waters (Moonwater, Rainwater, Snow Water and the rest) are ordinary ingredients, always on the shelf; nothing is gathered. Sea Water too: nothing is counted. Morwenna no longer brings a bottle, and nothing says she does: Hester's letter now says every bottle stays full, the sea included, and Morwenna sets down her lantern instead.

## Books and words

- Nine books, each opening on an index; one entry per page; up to 5 coloured bookmarks; search on the index only.
- Each entry shows a fixed "slightly hard" choice of keywords; the rest are blanks. Recipe numbers are hidden.
- Nothing goes into the word bank automatically: dotted words in any text are tapped to collect. A word leaves the bank once every blank that needs it is filled.
- Every ingredient and tarot card has a short **look** shown where its picture will go (also the art brief).
- A book opens as a book: its closed cover first, which swings open by itself (or at a click) onto the open spread; then two pages at a time, one entry per page, the index first (over as many pages as it needs). Pages turn with an animation (arrows beside the book, or the arrow keys); the turning sheet bends like paper, its free edge lagging behind. The cover is a board with some thickness (its edge shows as it swings). Fur covers (Recipe Book, Gentle Bestiary) have a solid core in the fur's colour behind the fur, so the pages never show through the gaps between the fur tips. A ribbon at the top of each entry page bookmarks it. The word bank sits beside the book.

## Letters

- 3 starting letters from "H." (Hester), plus **17 post letters** from places around the map, one or two hints each.
- Letters only point at **dead ends**: ingredients whose needed keyword is hidden, so a keyword search can't find them. They name the ingredient and the keyword, and carry a light thread to the order they help, but never say which order.
- In the game the letters arrive as **email**, read on the computer in **Moonpost**, a made-up webmail (Inbox, Archive; sender, subject, preview, date). The design document still calls them letters.
- An email arrives in a quiet hour with an envelope animation (Read it now / Later); unread emails show a badge on the computer.
- Emails can be archived and moved back to the inbox.

## The counter screen

The game has two full-screen views:
- **The counter** (main): the shop picture fills the window. Day info top left, what the customer says in a box over the right-hand window, the mood line as a subtitle, and a **down arrow** at the bottom: the counter and the altar are like two floors, and the arrow slides the view down to the altar (an up arrow slides back).
- **The workroom** (down arrow): one picture (assets/scene/altar-room.jpg, 2752×1536, two cabinets of doors and a pink-clothed table). It stands on the bottom of the window, so the table and the bar on its cloth are always visible on any screen; a wide window crops a little ceiling, then shows dark wall at the sides, no Kitchen/Altar/Cabinet split. Behind each of the 16 painted cupboard doors is one kind of ingredient, its name written in ink on the painted brass label (left cabinet: Herbs, Spices, Fruits, Pantry, Waters, Oils, Resins, Inks; right: Candles, Tarot, Crystals, Critters (animal gifts), Buttons, Threads, Wood, Findings). The bottom row of doors is hidden by the table and stays shut. Hovering a door eases it open a crack with pink light behind; clicking swings it open and its contents come out between the cabinets. Its label has a soft, wide pink glow while any of its things are on the table. Empty bottles that can be filled right now are at the top of the Waters cupboard. The table is the altar: the same stack of books as on the counter stands at its left end (click a book to open it), then the candle stand, always there (the designer's cut-out, assets/candles/candle-stand.png; click it empty to open the Candles cupboard), with the chosen candle standing in its cup behind the front lip (the designer's candle pictures, assets/candles, one per colour; the Candles cupboard shows them too), what is laid out in the middle (the ingredients float a little above the cloth, bobbing gently and a little out of step, their shadows on the cloth; still when the player has reduced motion), and the tarot card lies flat and wholly on the cloth, in the smooth patch between the two folds on the right, its far edge leaning towards the middle of the room as a flat card would, with the black test-candle candelabra beside it at the right end; recipe choice and the buttons sit along its front edge. A test result shows between the cabinets.

On the counter, every object glows on hover and does something:
- each book in the stack opens that book
- crystal ball: glows with the current customer's aura. It is not a button; Hester's first letter (P.S.) says what each colour means.
- till: today's receipts on their own over the shop (no panel, no dimming); a click anywhere puts them away
- notebook: my notes, written by the player: it opens straight onto its ruled pages (assets/books/diary-open.png, no cover), typing goes onto the lines in Caveat, a page holds what fits its lines, there is always a blank spread after the last page written on; turned like the other books; no tabs, search, bookmarks or word bank. (Hints moved to the reviews; what the notebook is for may be looked at again.)
- bell: the next customer
- computer: opens like a real computer: a monitor with a thin dark bezel; the designer's wallpaper (assets/scene/desktop-background.jpg) with two app icons, **Moonpost** (email; its icon an envelope sealed with a crescent moon) and **My Shop** (reviews and rating; its icon a little shopfront with a striped awning; inside, the shop's name and winged-heart logo head the side panel). A click on an icon opens its window, which grows out of the icon; windows have a title bar (move it by dragging, double-click to maximise) and minimise, maximise and close buttons, and shrink back into the icon (or the taskbar) when closed (minimised). The taskbar shows show-the-desktop, only the apps that are open, and on the right the tray and the date (Day n, weekday, and the festival, full moon or sky event). The badge on the computer counts unread emails and new reviews.
- **Crystal ball at rest**: the pink and purple swirl inside the glass is turned nearly white (the same picture, its swirl colour taken out and lifted toward white, a faint trace of its shape left; stars, glass edge and stand unchanged), so its own colour can't be mistaken for a customer's aura. While an aura colours it, the ball as drawn (assets/scene/crystal-ball-swirl.png, swirl and all) fades in over the white one, so the colour comes in with a clear swirl.
- **Logo**: the designer's winged heart in its silver frame (assets/scene/logo.png). For the shop (My Shop on the computer, and the tab icon, assets/scene/favicon.png, 128px square) a flat version in pinks of one hue, made from the same drawing (assets/scene/logo-flat.png): the wings white with a thicker #B03A7E outline (their feather lines kept), the heart as drawn with flat pinks (#E27BB1, its lines #B03A7E), an even light rim (#FBDDEB) and a clean outer line, the star on top as a flat shape (the shield's #F2A9C9, with an even #B03A7E outline), and the shield, with its moons, one solid #F2A9C9 with no inner lines or decoration, edged with an even #B03A7E outline.
- **Sounds** (js/sound.js): made in code for now, no sound files: the candle blown out, and the shop bell rung for the next customer (a hand bell, ringing twice as it swings), and the altar's desk bell (one bright, higher "ding!" with a click of its striker), and the till printing the day's receipts as the closing card comes up (a few buzzing bursts, then the slip torn off; only when there were sales), and the shop door closing behind each customer as they come in (also when a new customer takes the place of one still lingering at the counter) ("kkiik... tak": the hinge creaking, then a short crisp knock and the latch), and a two-note notification chime ("di-ring") whenever something new comes in on the computer (the red badge goes up: an email, the night's reviews).
- **No top bar** over the shop: the date is on the computer and the rating in My Shop. The prototype bar (with Restart) stays while PROTOTYPE_TOOLS is on.

## Art direction (agreed so far)

- Shop background as a clean plate (shelves, windows, door, table). Interactive objects (till, computer, bell, crystal ball, open book, door) are separate transparent PNG layers with hover glow.
- Window view as its own layer so it can change with hour and season.
- Remove brand logos (the computer's Apple logo). Aim for 1920×1080 or larger.
- Workroom wall decoration: the designer's new wall decor was tried over the winged heart drawn into the room picture and taken out again (it did not sit naturally); the room picture's own decoration stays.
- Choosing what to make happens only in the Recipe Book: its kind in the index, then its name, then a tap on the name at the top of its page, which puts a handwritten tick beside it (tap again to unchoose). Two small solid one-colour icons sit at the right of the title line, with no words (the tutorial says what they do): the altar bell (its silhouette, with one thin ridge under the knob and one above the base) lays out what is written on the page; a round-bottomed bottle test crafts it. Both are the same height. The altar has no dropdowns: a subtitle on the table names what is being made ("Making … Cookies") and opens its page; the altar bell asks to choose in the Recipe Book first if nothing is chosen. Every title keeps a little room at its end for the tick, so choosing a recipe never changes how its title wraps; the icons sit snug (a little apart from each other), so only 9 long titles wrap to two lines. A short word (the, a, of, to, in, for, and…) never ends a line in a title: it goes down with the word after it ("Another Orbit Around" / "the Sun").

- The talk at the counter (balloon, buttons, advice box, tutorial note) is slightly see-through: 99% solid, 98% on the darkest nights.
- Recipe Book pages: the blanks are ruled lines ("Herb or flower ____"), not dropdowns. Tapping one unfolds a short text index right under it (above it if the page has no room), two columns, names only (the keywords are for the player to look up in the books); what is chosen is written in by hand (Caveat) and rubbed out with a tap. Hovering a blank only turns its label and line pink; the line sits where an underline would. Candle and Card each have their own row, every row's slots start in one column,. The recipe text is set a size larger than the other books' notes. No counts beside the headings; the headings (Themes, Ingredients, Candle, Card, When) are in the title serif, the rest in the reading font.
- While the day card shows at dawn, nothing can be clicked.

- Interface look: clean and cozy. Cream panels with soft shadows (no glows), thin solid lines, rounded corners, deep plum text and one fuchsia pink accent (#D9559E, with #B03A7E for keyword text so it reads on cream; chosen to stand out for notifications too; main buttons, selected tabs, keywords, badges; it was a berry pink, then lilac, then deep teal, then midnight blue, then cornflower blue, then coral, each changed for not looking right); twilight-sky page with small stars, hearts around window titles. ## Open items

- **Handwriting ink**: every handwritten thing in the game uses Hester's ink, #3A2A3A (a warm near-black): her notes, the words written into the Recipe Book's blanks and its tick, the notebook's pages, the parcel's tag. (Her notes' titles are plum, #7A2E5E.)
- **Coming back where you left off**: leaving the page (closing, reloading) or going back to the title keeps what was open in the save (S.ui): the counter or the workroom, the computer with its apps and the email being read, a book and its page, the phone, an open cupboard, the receipts, the end-of-day card.
